01

Topology optimization

Topology optimization finds the best distribution of material in a design space for a given objective and set of constraints. We develop new multiphysics and multi-objective formulations — and build on established ones — so that the optimized designs are both high-performing and printable. Applications range from aerospace and automotive brackets to heat exchangers and orthopaedic implants.

  • Multiphysics
  • Design-dependent loads
  • Graded porous infill
  • Large-scale / parallel computing
02

Lattice & porous structures

AM makes it possible to build lattice, porous and infill structures that were once impractical to produce. They offer excellent strength-to-weight ratios for load-bearing parts, and very large surface areas — especially triply periodic minimal surfaces (TPMS) — for heat transfer. We design lattices from periodic or random unit cells, invent new cell types, and couple them with topology optimization through infill-based approaches. Uses include lightweighting, metamaterials, bone scaffolds and impact absorbers.

  • Strut, plate & TPMS lattices
  • Implicit modelling
  • Functional grading
  • Mechanical characterization
03

Thermal–fluid design

Many of our problems are multifunctional: a part has to carry load and move heat. We design liquid-cooled heat sinks and heat exchangers for electronics and energy systems by combining thermal-fluid topology optimization with conformal minimal-surface lattices, and we test the designs through simulation and printed prototypes — including through industry collaborations and the ITherm student cold-plate challenge.

  • Heat sinks & cold plates
  • TPMS heat exchangers
  • CFD
  • Electrochemical AM
04

Process modelling & constraints

A design is only useful if it can be made. We model the printing process to predict how a part deforms and what residual stresses build up during and after printing, and we develop fast small- and large-scale process models — such as inherent-strain methods — that can run inside an optimization loop. Capturing the process in the design method lets us avoid severe defects before anything is printed, for laser powder bed fusion and wire-arc AM alike.

  • Residual stress & distortion
  • Inherent strain method
  • Support structure design
  • Wire-arc AM
05

AI-assisted design & process optimization

Convolutional neural networks and generative adversarial networks have been used to speed up topology optimization, and there is still a lot of room to improve. We are developing data-driven surrogate models that learn from process data — for example, predicting hot and cold spots from photodiode or optical tomography signals — and feed those predictions back into topology optimization. The same tools help us choose process parameters, as in our work on wire-arc AM.

  • Surrogate models
  • Deep learning for topology optimization
  • In-situ monitoring data
  • Parameter optimization
06

Open software

A core aim of the lab is to put new design techniques in the hands of researchers, teachers, engineers and designers. We release our tools — mostly open source — for topology optimization, fast AM process modelling, implicit lattice modelling and design decision-making, and we use the feedback to improve them.
